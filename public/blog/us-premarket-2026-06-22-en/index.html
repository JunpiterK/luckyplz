Today's Pre-Market Verdict

U.S. equity futures are trading near flat ahead of the Monday open, digesting Thursday's strong session that saw the S&P 500 and Nasdaq surge on semiconductor strength and falling oil prices. The market's three-day break for the long weekend has kept futures muted, but the technical setup favors continuation of the risk-on tone established late last week. The catalyst stack remains positive: the U.S.-Iran peace memorandum signed Friday is holding, WTI has stabilized below $77, and semiconductor momentum from Intel's Apple partnership announcement continues to reverberate through the tech complex. With no economic data scheduled for today, traders will focus on sector rotation dynamics and positioning ahead of Thursday's critical May PCE inflation report, which will be the first major test of whether the peace dividend can offset sticky core price pressures.

📊 The Defining Catalyst — U.S.-Iran Peace Deal Shifts Market Calculus

The preliminary U.S.-Iran agreement, signed Friday in Geneva, marks the most significant geopolitical development of 2026 and has fundamentally altered the inflation and Fed policy narrative. The deal commits to reopening the Strait of Hormuz within 60 days and lifting the U.S. naval blockade, which had disrupted roughly 20% of global oil flows since late February. WTI crude, which spiked above $120 in May, has now retreated to $76.54, a three-month low. This collapse in energy prices drove Thursday's equity rally, with the S&P 500 climbing +1.08% and the Nasdaq surging +1.91%, led by a +6.42% explosion in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index.

The Oil Plunge and Inflation Implications

Brent crude fell below $80 per barrel on Tuesday for the first time since March, and WTI followed suit. The energy shock that pushed May CPI to 4.2% year-over-year—the highest since April 2023—is now reversing. The question for markets is whether this decline will be rapid enough to prevent the Federal Reserve from hiking rates. Cleveland Fed inflation nowcasts suggest June CPI could moderate, but Thursday's May PCE report will be critical. Consensus expects headline PCE at +4.1% YoY (vs. 3.8% in April) and core PCE at +3.4% YoY (vs. 3.3%). If the actual prints come in line or softer, it would validate the market's bet that energy was the sole driver of the inflation spike and that the Fed can stay on hold. A hotter-than-expected reading, however, would force a reassessment of the rate path and could trigger a sharp reversal in equities.

Fed Chair Warsh's Hawkish Pivot

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting on June 17 delivered a policy hold as expected, but the accompanying dot plot shocked markets by showing 9 of 18 officials penciling in at least one rate hike by year-end. This marked a dramatic shift from the March projections, which had pointed to rate cuts. Warsh himself declined to submit a forecast, but his press conference language emphasized the Fed's "unambiguous" commitment to restoring 2% inflation. He also eliminated forward guidance, saying it was "not well-suited to the current policy conjuncture." Two-year Treasury yields spiked 16 basis points to 4.21% that afternoon, the largest one-day move in over a year. The 10-year yield settled at 4.45%, up from 4.18% the prior week. The market is now pricing a 35% probability of a 25bp hike at the September FOMC, up from near-zero before Warsh's meeting. This tightening bias is a headwind for equities, but it has been offset by the collapse in oil prices and the semiconductor rally.

Korean Market Implications

South Korean equities have been among the biggest beneficiaries of the peace deal and the AI semiconductor boom. The KOSPI closed Friday at 9,052, down just -0.13% despite renewed geopolitical jitters over cancelled U.S.-Iran talks in Geneva on Friday morning. The index had hit record highs above 9,300 earlier in the week, driven by Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which together account for over 42% of the KOSPI's weight. SK Hynix surged on news it had shipped samples of 12-layer HBM4E memory to key customers, while Samsung gained on broader AI infrastructure optimism. Korean officials have noted that the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz could stabilize global supply chains and reduce external uncertainties facing the export-driven economy. However, foreign investors remain cautious on valuation, with the KOSPI trading at elevated multiples after its 190% rally from the 2024 lows. Monday's Korean session will be closely watched for whether the peace dividend can sustain momentum or if profit-taking accelerates.

Bottom Line — Positioning & Strategy

The market is in a tug-of-war between two narratives: the bullish "peace dividend" story (lower oil = lower inflation = no Fed hikes) versus the hawkish "Warsh put is gone" story (Fed credibility requires tightening even if oil falls). For now, the bulls are winning, as evidenced by Thursday's broad-based rally and Friday's resilient close despite some late-day wobbles. The path of least resistance for Monday's session is a modest extension of gains, particularly in semiconductors, which have the momentum and a clear AI demand tailwind. Energy stocks will remain under pressure, creating a barbell dynamic where tech and consumer discretionary outperform while energy and materials lag. The key risk is that Thursday's PCE report surprises to the upside, which would reignite inflation fears and force the market to price in a September hike. Until then, traders should favor long positions in semiconductor leaders (NVDA, AVGO, INTC) and short exposure to energy (XLE). Keep stop-losses tight, as the VIX has been creeping higher despite the equity rally, signaling underlying nervousness about the durability of the peace agreement and the Fed's reaction function.
