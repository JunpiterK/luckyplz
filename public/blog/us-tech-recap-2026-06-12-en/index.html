📊 Today's Core Theme — Rotation Into Financials and Metals

Thursday's session was defined by a pronounced sector rotation away from mega-cap tech into financials, materials, and defensive sectors. The catalyst was twofold: rising long-term Treasury yields boosted bank profitability outlooks, while escalating geopolitical tensions drove a flight to precious metals. Apple's 1.52% decline and Amazon's 1.23% drop underscored the pressure on growth stocks, yet the broader market shrugged off the weakness as cyclical leadership took over.

Financials Surge on Rising Yields

XLF jumped 1.37% as the 10Y Treasury yield climbed 0.54% to 4.49%, steepening the yield curve and improving net interest margin outlooks for major banks. JPMorgan gained 3.2% and Bank of America rose 2.9%, with analysts noting that the 2s10s spread expansion supports lending profitability. The move suggests investors are pricing in sustained higher rates and a more favorable environment for traditional banking operations.

Precious Metals Explode on Geopolitical Fears

Gold surged 3.52% to $4234.10 and silver skyrocketed 6.52% to $68.05, marking one of the sharpest single-day rallies in months. Reports of escalating Middle East tensions drove safe-haven flows, with Newmont and Freeport-McMoRan rallying 4.9% and 5.8% respectively. The metals rally contrasted starkly with WTI crude's 3.80% plunge to $84.38, as demand concerns outweighed geopolitical supply risks. This divergence highlights uncertainty about global growth trajectories even as risk-off sentiment dominates.

Korean Market Implications

The financials rally and rising 10Y yields suggest Korean banks and insurance stocks may benefit from a similar yield backdrop if Korean government bond yields follow suit. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix face headwinds from Apple's China-driven weakness, while POSCO and steel names could see support from the materials sector strength. Watch for potential rotation into Korean value plays if the US trend persists into next week.
