▸ The Five Rally Drivers
DRIVER 01

AI Capex Acceleration

Combined capex of the four hyperscalers (MSFT/Google/Meta/Amazon) hits $1.2T in 2026 (+52% YoY), up from $790B in 2025. The "AI capex peak" thesis the market feared has collapsed — instead capex is reaccelerating. Meta alone is at $200B; AWS Trainium silicon adds another $80B separately. Even when capex flows to custom ASICs instead of NVDA, memory and foundry demand stay the same.

DRIVER 02

HBM Supercycle + 12-Month Lead Time

HBM3E (8/12-Hi) is the lifeblood of NVDA Blackwell/Rubin and AMD MI350/MI400. Supply share: SK hynix 52%, Samsung 28%, Micron 20%. SK hynix is sold out through 2027 under full-capacity contracts with NVDA. Samsung's HBM3E 12-Hi cleared NVIDIA qualification in late 2025, ramping shipments in 2026. HBM ASP is 5× standard DRAM — memory company operating margins are exploding (SK hynix Q1 2026 estimated 40%+ operating margin).

DRIVER 03

Sovereign AI — National GPU Procurement

Non-US governments are buying NVDA H200/B200 directly for national AI infrastructure under the "AI sovereignty" banner: Saudi Arabia (HUMAIN, $40B), UAE (G42), Japan (SoftBank), India (Reliance), Korea (Naver-Saudi consortium). Sovereign AI demand reaches $150B+ in 2026 — 3× the 2025 level of ~$50B. This is government budget spending, not capex-sensitive — insensitive to macro slowdown.

DRIVER 04

Custom ASIC Trend — Broadcom's Breakout

Google TPU v6 (Trillium), AWS Trainium2/3, Meta MTIA v2, MSFT Maia — all designed and manufactured through Broadcom. Broadcom AI revenue: $4B in 2023 → estimated $32B in 2026 (8×). NVDA GPUs win on generality; ASICs win 2-3× efficiency on specific workloads. This is non-zero-sum — both grow. Both also consume HBM and foundry capacity, so memory and TSMC benefit either way.

DRIVER 05

TSMC Capacity + 2nm Ramp

TSMC 3nm lines at 100% utilization; 2nm production starts H2 2026. NVDA/AMD/AAPL/MediaTek are competing to secure 2nm allocation. Samsung Foundry's GAA 3nm yield is improving — winning Tesla Dojo and some Qualcomm orders. Foundry pricing + scarcity = TSMC revenue +40% in 2026, Samsung System LSI share rebounding.

▸ Four Key Risks
R1. AI ROI Skepticism
If $1.2T hyperscaler capex doesn't translate to revenue, capex guidance could be cut in H2 2026. OpenAI + Anthropic combined revenue is ~$50B — just 4% of capex. The market is starting to question this gap.
R2. China Export Controls / Retaliation
US extending HBM3E export bans to China would hit NVDA and SK hynix revenue 5-10% immediately. Chinese retaliation via rare earth / gallium export controls would shock the entire semiconductor supply chain.
R3. Memory Cycle Normalization
Outside HBM, standard DRAM and NAND supply may normalize by early 2027 — ASP declines could follow. China's CXMT and YMTC accelerate if they hit 28nm production scale on DDR5.
R4. Geopolitics — Taiwan / Korean Peninsula
Taiwan blockade/invasion scenarios would instantly destroy 50%+ of TSMC value and paralyze the global semiconductor supply chain. North Korean provocations or peninsula tensions apply a soft discount to Korean memory duo.

BOTTOM LINE

The current rally is closer to normal pricing of structural demand + supply shortage than to an "AI bubble". The key variables: HBM 12-month lead time, TSMC 2-3nm at full utilization, and the durability of $1.2T capex. In the base case (50% probability) Korean duo could add +30-50% and US big names +20-40%. Bull case (25%) sees another double through 2027. Bear case (25%) — capex guidance cuts + ROI skepticism — implies 20-30% correction from current.

Indicators to watch: ① Quarterly HBM pricing ② Hyperscaler capex guidance ③ TSMC 2nm utilization ④ NVDA Rubin shipment ramp ⑤ Sovereign AI new contracts.
