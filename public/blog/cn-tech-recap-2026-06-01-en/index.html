Today's Verdict

Mainland China equities closed under pressure Monday as the CSI 300 shed 0.98% to 4,844.26, dragged by semiconductor losses and persistent northbound capital outflows totaling approximately 85 billion yuan. The tech-heavy Shenzhen Component fell 1.51% while the SSE Composite declined a more modest 0.27%. Hong Kong diverged sharply, with the Hang Seng gaining 0.82% to 25,389.11 and the Hang Seng Tech Index surging 1.85%, powered by Tencent's 2.25% gain and Meituan's 6.4% rally on an expanded buyback announcement. Northbound selling marked the third straight session, with foreign investors rotating out of semiconductors (SMIC -22 billion yuan, Will Semi -15 billion yuan) and into defensive consumer names like Moutai and Wuliangye. The bifurcation between A-shares and Hong Kong underscores ongoing risk-off sentiment on the Mainland versus offshore optimism around quality tech at attractive valuations.

📊 Today's Defining Theme — Northbound Capital Exodus Extends to Third Day

The dominant narrative Monday was the continued flight of foreign capital from A-shares via the Stock Connect channels. Net northbound outflows reached approximately 85 billion yuan, bringing the three-day cumulative exodus to over 250 billion yuan—the most sustained selling streak since early April. This wave of foreign selling coincides with semiconductor sector weakness and broader macro caution around US-China tech tensions.

Sector Rotation: Out of Chips, Into Liquor

Northbound investors dumped semiconductor exposure aggressively: SMIC's A-share listing saw 22 billion yuan in net selling, Will Semiconductor 15 billion yuan, and Unigroup Guoxin 12 billion yuan. Conversely, defensive plays gained favor—Kweichow Moutai attracted 18 billion yuan, Wuliangye 12 billion yuan. This rotation signals a shift from cyclical growth bets to quality defensives amid valuation and geopolitical concerns. SMIC's 3.25% drop came after a two-week rally that had lifted the stock 18%, triggering profit-taking as US export control headlines resurfaced over the weekend.

Hong Kong Divergence: Southbound Inflows Cushion Offshore Market

While Mainland investors fled A-shares, southbound flows into Hong Kong totaled approximately 95 billion yuan, with Meituan alone absorbing 35 billion yuan and Tencent 28 billion yuan. This cross-border arbitrage reflects Mainland capital seeking quality tech exposure at H-share discounts and highlights structural demand for Hong Kong's offshore liquidity and regulatory premium. Meituan's buyback expansion to HK$10 billion catalyzed the 6.4% surge, validating the offshore haven trade. The spread between Hang Seng Tech (+1.85%) and CSI 300 (-0.98%) widened to 283 basis points, the largest intraday gap in three weeks.

Bottom Line

Mainland A-shares face technical resistance as northbound selling pressure persists for a third session, with cumulative outflows exceeding 250 billion yuan. Semiconductor exposure remains vulnerable to profit-taking and geopolitical headlines, while defensive consumer names offer near-term shelter. Hong Kong's relative strength, driven by southbound inflows and corporate buyback catalysts, suggests offshore listings provide better risk-reward for China exposure. Watch for PBOC liquidity signals and June trade data (due June 7) to gauge whether this risk-off rotation extends into mid-month.
