Today's Verdict

Wall Street opens higher Thursday, led by a ferocious semiconductor rally after Micron Technology delivered a blowout quarter that reaffirmed AI infrastructure demand remains insatiable. The memory-chip giant's fiscal Q3 earnings crushed estimates by 22.5%, with data center revenue exceeding $25 billion and gross margins hitting a record 84.9%. More critically, management announced 16 strategic customer agreements that lock in multi-year supply commitments, addressing the core fear that drove Tuesday's 8% SOX selloff: whether massive AI capex will translate into sustained chip demand. Qualcomm added fuel by forecasting $15 billion in data center chip sales by 2029, securing partnerships with Meta and Microsoft. NQ futures surge 2.15%, erasing half of this week's losses.

But the euphoria faces an immediate test at 08:30 ET when May PCE data drops. The Fed's preferred inflation metric is expected to show headline PCE at 4.1% YoY (up from 3.8%) and core PCE at 3.4% (from 3.3%), reflecting sticky services inflation despite collapsing oil prices. With new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh raising the dots at last week's meeting and signaling the next move could be a hike, a hot PCE print would validate the hawkish pivot and pressure rate-cut expectations. The 10Y yield sits at 4.39% after Wednesday's -1.42% drop on falling oil; any upside inflation surprise sends it back toward 4.60% and threatens today's tech rally.

📊 Today's Catalyst — Micron's Memory Supercycle Thesis

Micron's after-hours surge from $1,047 to $1,200 (+14.6%) represents a $180 billion market cap swing that reversed three days of sector-wide carnage. Tuesday's tech rout — Nasdaq -3.3%, MU -13.2%, QCOM -8% — reflected mounting skepticism that hyperscaler AI spending would sustain chip demand through 2027. SK Hynix's decision to slow advanced AI chip production in favor of commodity DRAM seemed to confirm peaking demand. Micron obliterated that narrative.

The Numbers That Changed the Game

Revenue jumped to $41.46 billion, 74% above consensus and up 346% year-over-year, marking the fifth consecutive quarterly record. Non-GAAP EPS hit $25.11 versus the $20.49 estimate, driven by an 84.9% gross margin — the highest in company history. Data center SSD revenue alone exceeded $5 billion, more than doubling sequentially. Operating cash flow reached $25.4 billion with free cash flow of $18.3 billion, also a quarterly record. Most importantly, Micron guided Q4 revenue to approximately $50 billion (versus $42 billion consensus), signaling no slowdown ahead.

Strategic Customer Agreements: The Structural Shift

The revelation that shook premarket trading: Micron has signed 16 strategic customer agreements (SCAs) — multi-year, take-or-pay contracts with hyperscalers that guarantee demand and pricing floors. Management stated these deals will fundamentally transform the business model, eliminating the boom-bust volatility that has plagued memory chips for decades. The company expects tight DRAM and NAND supply conditions to persist beyond calendar 2027 due to AI-driven demand coupled with structural supply constraints. This directly contradicts the bear case that drove Tuesday's selloff.

Korean Market Implications

Friday's Korean open will hinge on whether PCE data allows the US rally to hold or forces a rotation back to defensives.
