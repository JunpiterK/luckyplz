One-paragraph take — May 15 reshaped a whole chapter of Korean markets in a single day. "All-time intraday high above 8,000 → -6.12% close same day" is a V-reversal you don't normally see, driven by 5.6T won of foreign net selling, with the market's circuit breaker triggering for the second time in a month. The US closed -1% across the board the same day, and over the weekend, Warsh's hawkish Fed confirmation, the underwhelming Trump-Xi summit, the US-Iran MOU stall, oil volatility, and a one-year-high 10Y at 4.59% all stacked. The May 18 open is a head-on collision between "oversold bounce" and "8th day of foreign selling + macro overhang." Average historical recovery from a circuit breaker is 11 days, but with variables still alive, the statistic shouldn't be a green light alone. The first 30 minutes of foreign flow determines today's intraday trend.

▸ Today's Must-check Risks
R1. Foreign selling on its 7th straight day — if it doesn't break on day 8, it's a trend
Foreigners have net-sold KOSPI for 7 straight sessions from May 7 to May 15, dumping 5.6T won on May 15 alone. If day-8 selling holds on May 18, this becomes a short-term trend. Welcome a gap-up open, but verify foreign-flow continuation in the 09:00~09:30 window. No flip-to-buy signal = no chasing.
R2. If Samsung & SK hynix wobble together, the index wobbles with them
Together accounting for 50%+ of KOSPI 200, both fell -7%+ and -6%+ on May 15. With that concentration risk intact, if both names look weak at the open, an index rebound is essentially impossible. Conversely, both at +1%+ takes the index up with them.
R3. Statistics only work when macro variables are absent
Seoul Economic Daily cites an average post-circuit-breaker recovery of 11 days and +9.9% in 32 days. Today's environment, however, has Warsh hawkish confirmation + Trump-Xi flop + US-Iran stall + 10Y one-year high all alive simultaneously. Those statistics are macro-quiet averages and shouldn't be applied directly to current conditions. Don't go all-in on the stat alone.
R4. US May 18 volatility also transmits to Korea
US May 18 regular session opens at 22:30 KST, after Korea closes. With OPEX aftermath + a likely first Warsh public appearance + alive Iran headlines, US volatility will be elevated. Even a strong Korea close can be reversed by a weak US 5/18 → Korea gap-down on 5/19. Same-day US news transmits straight to Korea's next session — keep that in mind.

BOTTOM LINE · One Line for Today

Korea today is a head-on collision between "oversold bounce trigger vs. day-8 foreign selling + macro triple punch." May 15's -6.12% creates a technical bounce setup, but the seller has to stop for a real rebound to land. With the US also down on May 15 and weekend variables unresolved, simple statistical bets are risky.

Five checkpoints: ① First 30 minutes — do foreigners go for day 8 of selling? (most decisive). ② Samsung & SK hynix open direction — both +1%+ enables an index rebound; otherwise range-bound. ③ KRW 1,490~1,500 zone — short-term exporter buying triggers? ④ After Korea closes, the US May 18 session — Warsh's first remarks and BIDU earnings flow into Korea's 5/19 gap. ⑤ Iran/WTI headlines — a re-break above $105 pressures risk assets broadly.

Traps to avoid: ① No "statistically due for a rebound" bets — averages lag while macro variables are alive. ② Don't chase pure low-buying of May 15 decliners — another foreign-selling round can take prices lower. ③ Margin-loan balances are near all-time highs; further declines accelerate via forced selling. It's a day to watch "are foreigners flipping to buyers," not the index number.
