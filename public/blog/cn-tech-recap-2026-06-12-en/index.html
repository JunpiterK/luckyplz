Today's Verdict

Mainland and Hong Kong markets delivered a broad-based rally Thursday, reversing three days of foreign selling pressure as policy stimulus hopes gained traction ahead of weekend macro data releases. CSI 300 climbed 1.16% to 4,777.32 while Hang Seng surged 1.61% to 24,639.98, driven by a 38 billion yuan northbound inflow that rotated into consumer discretionary and financials. Tencent's Q1 gaming revenue beat and BYD's 28% year-over-year NEV sales growth in May anchored Hong Kong gains, offsetting semiconductor weakness led by SMIC's 2.46% decline on US export restriction concerns. The NDRC's signal of accelerated infrastructure approvals in H2 combined with softer-than-expected May CPI data reinforced expectations for continued monetary easing, supporting risk appetite across A-shares and HK stocks despite persistent demand concerns.

📊 Today's Defining Theme — Northbound Reversal Fuels Consumer and Financial Rotation

The standout narrative Thursday was the sharp reversal in northbound capital flows, which turned net positive at 38 billion yuan after three consecutive days of outflows totaling 120 billion yuan. This marked shift coincided with NDRC comments on accelerated infrastructure spending and soft May CPI data, prompting foreign investors to rebuild positions in consumer discretionary and financials ahead of potential policy catalysts.

Northbound Buying Concentrated in Premium Consumption

Kweichow Moutai absorbed 12.5 billion yuan of northbound buying, driving the stock up 3.42% to ¥1,682.50 on expectations of robust baijiu demand ahead of the Dragon Boat Festival. Midea Group attracted 8.3 billion yuan on rumors of expanded home appliance subsidies, gaining 2.89% to ¥78.45. China Merchants Bank and Ping An Insurance pulled in 6.7 billion yuan and 5.9 billion yuan respectively, reflecting confidence in net interest margin stabilization and May life insurance premium growth. The rotation into consumption and financials contrasted sharply with semiconductor outflows, where SMIC, Zhongwei, and Hua Hong saw combined selling of 9.4 billion yuan on US export restriction worries.

Hong Kong Tech Strength Despite ADR Weakness

Tencent's 1.31% gain to HK$463.20 and Xiaomi's 1.39% rise to HK$26.20 underscored divergence between Hong Kong and US-listed Chinese equities. While Tencent's Q1 gaming revenue beat drove southbound inflows of 18.2 billion yuan, Alibaba ADR fell 2.33% to $112.69 and XPeng dropped 2.69% to $14.46 in New York on profit-taking and delivery guidance concerns. BYD's 1.53% advance to HK$86.25, supported by 14.7 billion yuan of southbound buying, reinforced the NEV sector's resilience despite margin pressure from raw material costs hitting suppliers like BYD Electronic and CATL.

Policy Catalyst Setup for June Data Week

May CPI's 0.3% year-over-year print, below the 0.5% consensus, combined with NDRC's infrastructure acceleration signal, has positioned markets for potential PBOC rate cuts or reserve requirement ratio reductions before month-end. Traders are now watching Friday's industrial production and retail sales data as the final inputs for June monetary policy decisions. The northbound reversal suggests institutional investors are front-running stimulus announcements rather than waiting for confirmation, a pattern that historically precedes 1-2 week rallies in A-shares and Hong Kong tech.
