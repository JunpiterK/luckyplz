But KOSDAQ crumbled -2.29% to 1,026.03 as the battery sector imploded—Samsung SDI -7.67%, Ecopro BM -4.35%, LG Energy Solution -2.75%—on fears of slowing EV demand and intensifying Chinese competition. 📊 Core Theme — Battery Sector Collapse vs Semiconductor Resilience

June 2nd exposed a sharp divergence within Korean growth stocks. The battery/secondary battery complex suffered its worst single-day rout in three months, while the semiconductor/IT platform segment absorbed foreign buying and rallied. This bifurcation reflects mounting skepticism over EV demand trajectories versus renewed confidence in AI chip infrastructure spending.

Battery Sector Implosion: EV Demand Doubts Resurface

Samsung SDI's -7.67% plunge to 602,000 won led a brutal selloff across the battery value chain. Foreign investors liquidated 185 billion won in Samsung SDI alone, the largest single-stock outflow of the day. Ecopro BM dropped -4.35%, POSCO Future M fell -3.68%, and LG Energy Solution shed -2.75%. The catalyst: overlapping concerns about decelerating global EV sales growth, rising Chinese battery maker competition with aggressive pricing, and potential delays in US IRA subsidy disbursements. Korean battery makers, which had priced in a multi-year EV super-cycle, now face margin compression and order pushouts. Institutional investors cut battery sector weights by 1.3 trillion won net over the past five sessions, according to KRX data.

Semiconductor Strength: AI Chip Demand Narrative Holds

In stark contrast, Samsung Electronics rallied +3.30% to 360,500 won on combined foreign and institutional buying totaling 206 billion won. The move reflects sustained optimism around HBM3E ramp-up and foundry order momentum from US hyperscalers. NAVER surged +3.31% to 280,500 won after announcing AI-powered search algorithm enhancements, attracting 48 billion won in foreign inflows. SK Hynix held flat at -0.13%, consolidating recent HBM-driven gains. The semiconductor and IT platform subsector posted a collective +2.1% gain, insulating KOSPI from broader weakness.

Foreign Flow Pattern: Sector Rotation Accelerates

Foreign investors executed aggressive sector rotation, buying 124 billion won in semiconductors while dumping 312 billion won in battery stocks. The top foreign buy was Samsung Electronics (+124 billion won), followed by NAVER (+48 billion won) and LG Chem (+32 billion won, bottom-fishing in battery materials). The top foreign sell was Samsung SDI (-185 billion won), followed by Hanwha Aerospace (-92 billion won, defense overvaluation concerns) and SK Hynix (-68 billion won, post-HBM profit-taking). Institutions mirrored the pattern, buying 82 billion won in Samsung Electronics and selling 64 billion won in Samsung SDI. The combined foreign-institutional exodus from batteries signals a structural reassessment of EV supply chain valuations.

Bottom Line

Korean equities are fragmenting along sector fault lines. Battery stocks face a multi-quarter reset as EV demand expectations moderate and Chinese competition intensifies—expect continued outflows until Q3 earnings provide a floor. Semiconductor and AI platform names remain preferred vehicles for Korea exposure, supported by credible HBM and hyperscaler capex narratives. Near-term KOSPI support sits at 8,750 (20-day MA); a break below risks accelerating foreign redemptions. KOSDAQ remains vulnerable sub-1,030, with battery and defense high-flyers (Hanwha Aerospace -5.98%) unwinding frothy valuations. Watch USD/KRW—a sustained breach above 1,520 would signal broader EM risk-off and compound selling pressure. Tactical approach: fade battery rallies, accumulate semiconductor dips on HBM themes, and maintain tight stops on defense names.
