Today's Verdict

China markets suffered a sharp technology-led selloff Thursday as semiconductor and EV stocks collapsed under the weight of US export control fears and intensifying price competition. The CSI 300 fell 1.48% to 4,868.22 while the Shenzhen Component plunged 3.44% to 15,782.22, with SMIC's 6.74% crash leading the semiconductor rout. Hong Kong mirrored the weakness as Alibaba ADR dropped 4.74%, BYD slid 4.34%, and the Hang Seng declined 1.79% to 22,663.82. Northbound capital executed a defensive rotation, dumping 12.5 billion yuan from SMIC while accumulating 8.2 billion yuan in Kweichow Moutai, signaling a clear flight to quality amid geopolitical and competitive headwinds. The session reveals investor preference for defensive consumer names over cyclical tech exposure as external risks mount.

📊 Today's Defining Theme — Semiconductor Panic on Export Control Fears

The dominant narrative driving today's selloff was the sharp escalation of US-China tech tensions, with reports indicating US authorities are evaluating broader semiconductor export restrictions targeting advanced chipmaking equipment. This sent shockwaves through the semiconductor supply chain and triggered the session's heaviest losses.

SMIC Leads Chip Sector Collapse

SMIC cratered 6.74% to HK$80.20 as investors priced in heightened risk of equipment access cuts that could cripple capacity expansion plans. The stock saw the heaviest northbound outflow of the day at 12.5 billion yuan, reflecting both foreign and domestic institutional capitulation. Related names including Hua Hong Semiconductor and AMEC also suffered significant declines, with the broader semiconductor theme down 5.2% for the session.

Policy Stability Fails to Offset Tech Headwinds

The People's Bank of China maintained the one-year MLF rate at 2.50%, signaling stable monetary policy, but the reassurance did little to counter the tech selloff. Defensive rotation accelerated into baijiu and banking names, with Kweichow Moutai gaining 1.8% on 8.2 billion yuan of northbound buying and China Merchants Bank advancing 0.7% on 3.8 billion yuan of inflows. The divergence underscores investor preference for earnings stability and dividend yield over growth exposure in the current environment.
