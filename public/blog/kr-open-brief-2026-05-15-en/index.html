Cisco's strong earnings, the US-China summit tone, and NVIDIA's H200 China export clearance combined to push the Dow back over 50,000, while the S&P and Nasdaq printed fresh record closes. Korea is right there too — KOSPI hit a record 7,981 on May 14, just shy of 8,000. Today's open has the tailwind to attempt a break of 8,000, but there's one crack in the story: foreign investors have actually been heavy net sellers of the large-cap chips. It's a "record index, split flows" regime — whether the gap-up open becomes a trend or gets capped by profit-taking is today's key question.

▸ Must-Check Risks for Today
R1. "Record index ≠ foreign buying"
KOSPI is at a record, but foreign investors net-sold SK hynix and Samsung in the trillions of won in May. The force lifting the index and the hands selling the large caps point in different directions — don't assume a gap-up open is a trend; confirm participant flows (foreign/institutional) within the first 30 minutes.
R2. Samsung + SK hynix = 51.5% of KOSPI 200
Two stocks make up over half of the KOSPI 200 index (as of May 13). If the two chip names wobble, the whole index wobbles with them — a concentration risk structure. Always keep in mind this is not a diversified market.
R3. Retail margin balances at a record = overheating signal
Reporting indicates Korean retail margin-loan balances have hit a record high amid the rally. A leveraged market amplifies drawdowns through forced liquidations when it falls. Set your volatility-management and stop-loss rules in advance.
R4. US May options expiration (OPEX) tonight
The US session that opens after the Korean close is the May standard options expiration. Expiration unwind can widen US intraday volatility, which gaps into the next Korean session (Monday). Trim size on positions carried over the weekend.
