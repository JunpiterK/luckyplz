Today's Verdict

China's equity markets extended losses on May 27 as foreign capital continued to exit through the northbound channel for the third consecutive session, draining approximately 8.2 billion yuan from Mainland stocks. The CSI 300 fell 0.80% while the Shanghai Composite dropped 1.25%, with selling concentrated in previously hot EV and consumer discretionary names. Hong Kong underperformed, with the Hang Seng down 1.06% and the tech-heavy sub-index losing 2.14%. The sharp 3.83% drop in WTI crude to $90.29 pressured energy shares and amplified concerns about slowing industrial demand. Despite the PBOC holding its medium-term lending facility rate steady at 2.50%, markets found little support, reflecting investor caution ahead of end-month positioning and mixed April industrial profit data showing a 3.2% year-on-year decline.

📊 Today's Defining Theme — Northbound Capital Flight and EV Sector Correction

The dominant narrative today was the sustained outflow of northbound capital and the sharp reversal in electric vehicle stocks, which had been among the strongest performers in recent weeks. These two forces combined to create a risk-off tone across Greater China equities.

Northbound Outflows Accelerate

Foreign investors pulled approximately 8.2 billion yuan from Mainland A-shares through the Stock Connect program on May 27, marking the third consecutive day of net selling. Top sell targets included Wuliangye -8.5 billion yuan, China Merchants Bank -7.3 billion yuan, and Inner Mongolia Yili -6.1 billion yuan, suggesting broad rotation out of consumer and financials. Defensive accumulation was limited to Kweichow Moutai (+9.2 billion yuan) and Ping An Insurance (+6.8 billion yuan), signaling a flight to quality within a weak tape. The cumulative three-day outflow now exceeds 22 billion yuan, raising concerns about momentum exhaustion in the rally that began in April.

EV Complex Hits Air Pocket

Electric vehicle and related auto names suffered steep declines, with Xiaomi dropping 4.57% to HK$28.40 and BYD falling 3.15% to HK$90.70. Southbound flows turned sharply negative for these names, with Xiaomi seeing -6.7 billion yuan net selling and BYD -5.3 billion yuan. Profit-taking accelerated after supply chain reports suggested margin pressure and valuation concerns emerged following the sector's 20%+ gain over the prior month. Interestingly, XPeng bucked the trend with a +6.16% rally in ADR trading, supported by stronger-than-expected May delivery data, highlighting stock-specific divergence within the EV space.

Commodity Weakness and Policy Disappointment

The 3.83% collapse in WTI crude weighed heavily on sentiment, dragging energy and materials stocks lower and reinforcing fears of slowing Chinese industrial activity. Meanwhile, the PBOC's decision to hold the MLF rate at 2.50% was widely expected but offered no incremental stimulus, leaving investors without a near-term policy catalyst. April industrial profit data released over the weekend showed a 3.2% year-on-year decline, further clouding the demand outlook and validating the cautious stance from offshore capital.

BOTTOM LINE

China's equity market is at a crossroads. The three-day northbound outflow totaling over 22 billion yuan signals waning foreign appetite, while the EV sector's sharp reversal after a multi-week rally suggests profit-taking is replacing momentum chasing. With the PBOC offering no incremental policy support and commodity weakness reinforcing demand concerns, near-term catalysts are scarce. The May PMI print on May 28 and potential LPR adjustment on May 29 are critical inflection points. Until then, expect defensive positioning to dominate—favor quality defensives like insurance and select consumer staples over high-beta tech and EV names. Northbound flow direction will be the key tell for whether the April-May rally can regain footing or faces deeper retracement.
