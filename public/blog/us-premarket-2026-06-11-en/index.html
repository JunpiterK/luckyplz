📊 Today's Defining Catalyst — May PPI: 1.1% Spike Confirms Energy-Led Inflation, But Core Softens

The 08:30 ET Producer Price Index release is the single most decisive data point today, arriving one day after May CPI confirmed inflation hit a three-year high of 4.2%. May PPI surged 1.1% month-over-month, matching April's revised pace and significantly exceeding the Dow Jones consensus of 0.7%. On a 12-month basis, PPI jumped 6.5%, the largest annual increase since November 2022 and slightly above the 6.4% estimate. Goods prices for final demand posted a 2.8% monthly jump—the steepest since the series launched in December 2009—accounting for nearly four-fifths of the overall PPI gain. Within goods, energy was the chief culprit, advancing 10.7%, with a 23.4% wholesale surge in gasoline alone responsible for more than half of the goods-category increase. Diesel, jet fuel, plastic resins, industrial chemicals, and related petroleum-based products also registered gains. However, core PPI (excluding food and energy) rose 0.4%, below the 0.5% consensus, indicating that rising fuel prices are causing much of the inflationary burden rather than broad-based price pressures.

Market Reaction: Fed December Hike Bets Rise, 10Y Yields Retreat

Treasury yields initially spiked on the headline PPI beat but retreated slightly after the core print came in softer than expected. The 10-year yield traded near 4.53%, down -0.22% from Wednesday's close, while the 2-year yield stood at 3.63%, down -0.14%. Futures markets increased pricing for a potential Fed rate hike at the December FOMC meeting to approximately 35%, up from 25% pre-PPI. The softer core reading provides some relief, but the headline surge—combined with yesterday's 4.2% CPI—reinforces that the Fed faces persistent inflation driven by the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict's impact on oil markets. WTI crude rose +0.31% to $90.31, while Brent crude traded near $94.84, up +1.87%. DXY Dollar Index gained +0.20% to 100.15, reflecting safe-haven demand and expectations that the Fed will remain on hold longer than European peers.

For Korean markets opening tonight, the PPI surge signals rising import costs for energy-intensive manufacturers—particularly petrochemicals, steel, and automotive. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, already nursing losses from Wednesday's -4.52% KOSPI plunge, face dual headwinds: elevated input costs and weakening global demand as Fed tightening expectations rise. The won closed at 1532.92 per dollar, up +0.47%, and further depreciation is likely if the Fed maintains a hawkish stance while the Bank of Korea remains dovish. KOSPI foreign net selling hit -417.2 billion won on Wednesday, and institutional selling reached -17 billion won, signaling risk-off positioning. Tonight's KOSPI 200 futures will likely gap down further unless U.S. equities sustain the pre-market bounce into the regular session. Key sectors to watch: shipbuilders (energy cost pressure), refiners (gasoline margin compression), and semiconductors (global demand slowdown).
