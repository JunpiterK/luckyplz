📊 Today's Catalyst — May PPI at 08:30 ET

Today's market hinge is the May Producer Price Index release at 08:30 ET, a critical inflation gauge that could determine whether the Fed maintains its patient stance or signals renewed hawkishness at next week's FOMC meeting. April's PPI surged 1.4% MoM and 6.0% YoY, the hottest reading since December 2022, driven by energy costs and trade services margins. May's consensus expects modest cooling to the 5.0%-5.9% YoY range, but the overnight oil shock complicates that baseline. WTI's 3.24% surge to $91.06 following US strikes on Iran raises the specter of energy-driven producer cost inflation feeding into core measures, which strip out food and energy but capture second-round effects like transportation and manufacturing input costs.

Data Release Mechanics and Market Implications

The May PPI report will detail final demand prices across goods (expected +2.0% from April's elevated level) and services (consensus +1.2%). Energy sub-indices are the wildcard: April saw crude petroleum jump 11.3% MoM and diesel fuel surge 5.0%, and May likely captured only the early stages of the Iran conflict's oil price impact. If core PPI (ex-food, energy, trade) accelerates above April's 0.5% MoM pace, it would signal broadening price pressures beyond commodities. Bond markets have pre-positioned for this risk, with 10Y yields at 4.53% and 2Y at 3.63%, while VIX's spike to 22.05 reflects elevated tail risk around the print. Polymarket prediction markets show trader uncertainty: 47% probability for 5.0%-5.9% YoY vs. 42% for 6.0%-6.9%, essentially a coin flip.

Second-Order Effects: Fed Path and Sector Rotation

A PPI surprise above 6.0% YoY would likely push 10Y yields toward 4.60%-4.65%, pressuring equity multiples and forcing investors to reassess the Fed's June dot plot. Materials (XLB) and industrials (XLI) would face margin compression narratives, while energy (XLE) could paradoxically benefit from cost-push pricing power. Conversely, a benign print at or below 5.5% would validate soft-landing optimism, potentially sparking a short-covering rally in oversold tech names — though Tuesday's 2.60% SOX decline suggests residual positioning cleanup remains. The dollar (DXY at 99.88) and gold ($4,154 after -2.48% overnight) are coiled for sharp moves post-release.
