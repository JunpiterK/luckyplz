Today's Verdict

Korean equities suffered a historic shellacking as foreign investors executed their largest single-day sell program in over a year, dumping 1.8 trillion won across KOSPI and KOSDAQ. The KOSPI collapsed -5.81% to 8,411.21, erasing an entire month of gains, while KOSDAQ fell -4.10% to 851.37. The rout was led by semiconductors and batteries — SK Hynix plunged -8.36% on HBM demand slowdown fears, Samsung Electronics dropped -5.30%, and the entire secondary battery value chain crumbled as EV inventory glut concerns resurfaced. Institutions stepped in with 800 billion won of net buying but were overwhelmed by the foreign exodus. Trading volume surged to 28.4 trillion won as panic selling accelerated into the close. The only bright spot: USD/KRW edged down -0.26% to 1,538.79, suggesting foreign selling proceeds have not yet hit the FX market in force.

📊 Foreign Selling Tsunami — 1.8 Trillion Won Exodus

Today's session was defined by a coordinated foreign selling wave that wiped out Korea's largest-cap stocks. Foreign investors sold a net 1.5 trillion won in KOSPI and 300 billion won in KOSDAQ, the heaviest single-day outflow since early 2025. The selling was highly concentrated: SK Hynix absorbed 124 billion won of net selling, Samsung Electronics 82 billion won, LG Energy Solution 45 billion won, and EcoPro BM 38 billion won. The catalyst was a sharp recalibration of AI infrastructure spending expectations — reports suggesting major cloud providers are slowing HBM procurement triggered a re-rating of Korea's semiconductor export cycle. This was not profit-taking after a rally; this was a structural reassessment of earnings momentum.

Semiconductor Rout — HBM Demand Peak Concerns

SK Hynix collapsed -8.36% to ₩2,673,000, its worst single-day drop in eight months, as HBM high-bandwidth memory demand concerns exploded. Samsung Electronics fell -5.30% to ₩339,500. The trigger: multiple sell-side reports flagged that AI server buildouts are decelerating faster than expected, creating a risk that HBM inventory builds at foundries and that pricing power erodes in H2 2026. Foreign funds, who had been heavily overweight Korea on the AI-memory trade, are now unwinding aggressively. Institutional buyers tried to defend the ₩2,700,000 level in SK Hynix but were overwhelmed by the selling pressure.

Battery Sector Collapse — EV Inventory Overhang

The secondary battery value chain suffered indiscriminate selling. LG Energy Solution fell -5.82% to ₩331,500, Samsung SDI dropped -5.41% to ₩455,000, EcoPro BM plunged -7.15% to ₩133,700, and POSCO Future M declined -5.64% to ₩160,600. The narrative shift: EV demand growth is slowing globally, particularly in Europe and China, and battery inventory is piling up at automakers. North America IRA Inflation Reduction Act policy uncertainty added to the pressure. Cathode material prices are softening, and Chinese competition in the mid-tier battery segment is intensifying. Foreign investors sold battery stocks en masse, and institutions showed little appetite to defend.

📉 Bottom Line

This was a conviction sell-off, not noise. Foreign investors are repricing Korea's semiconductor and battery export cycles lower, and today's 1.8 trillion won outflow signals a structural shift, not a tactical blip. The KOSPI has now surrendered all gains from the past month, and technical support at 8,400 is under immediate threat. Institutions are attempting to defend large-cap semiconductors and batteries, but their buying power is insufficient against the foreign exodus. The only defense: USD/KRW held relatively firm at 1,538.79, down just -0.26%, suggesting repatriation flows have not yet accelerated. Watch for stabilization in HBM demand narratives and any signal from major cloud capex guidance. Until then, Korea's tech-heavy index faces further downside risk. The market is now in a prove-it phase — investors will demand concrete data confirming that AI infrastructure spending and EV demand are stabilizing before re-entering aggressively.
